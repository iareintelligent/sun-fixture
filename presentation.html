The Big Idea

What If Your Lights Acted Like the Sun?

Think of it like this

Imagine you have 8 light bulbs arranged in a circle on your ceiling — one pointing North, one Northeast, one East, and so on, like a compass.


Now imagine the real sun is in the East during the morning. Your East light shines the brightest, the lights next to it (NE and SE) are pretty bright too, and the West light is off — because the sun is on the other side of the sky!


As the sun moves across the sky during the day, the bright spot moves around your lights to match. It's like having a tiny sun inside your house!

The Hardware

What Parts Make This Work?

Think of it like a team — each piece has a job to do.

🧠

Home Assistant Green

The Brain. A small computer that runs everything. It talks to all the other devices and tells the lights what to do.

🌆

Philips Hue Bridge

The Translator. It speaks the lights' language (Zigbee radio signals) and passes along instructions from the brain.

💡

Hue Color Bulbs (x8)

The Performers. Smart bulbs that can be any color or brightness. Arranged in 8 compass directions (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW).

🔀

Lutron Aurora Dimmer

The Remote Control. A physical knob you can twist to make lights brighter or dimmer, and click to switch modes.

💡 Fun Fact: The lights talk to the Hue Bridge using invisible radio waves called Zigbee — kind of like how your phone uses Wi-Fi, but designed just for smart home gadgets!
Sun Tracking

How Does It Know Where the Sun Is?

Two Key Measurements

⬆️

Elevation (Height)

How high the sun is above the horizon. 0° means it's right at the horizon (sunrise/sunset). 90° means it's directly overhead (noon).

🧭

Azimuth (Direction)

Which compass direction the sun is in. Like a compass: 0° = North, 90° = East, 180° = South, 270° = West.

Think of it like this

Imagine you're standing in a field. To find the sun, you need two things:


1. Where to point — Do you look East? South? West? That's the azimuth (direction).


2. How high to look — Is it near the ground or way up high? That's the elevation (height).


Home Assistant figures out both of these using math and your location on Earth. It checks every 60 seconds!

⏱ Every 60 Seconds: The system checks the sun's position, calculates new brightness and color for each light, and updates them — all in less than a second!

What's a "Kelvin"?

Kelvin (K) is how we measure the color of white light. It's like a scale:


🔥 Low numbers (2000K) = Warm, orange, cozy — like a campfire or candle.

☀️ Medium (4000K) = Neutral, natural — like a cloudy day.

❄️ High numbers (6500K) = Cool, bright white — like being outside at noon.

⛅

Cloud Cover Effect

If it's cloudy outside, the system makes the lights a little warmer — because real cloudy skies look different than clear ones!

💧

Humidity Effect

At night, if there's moisture in the air, the moon looks warmer. The lights copy this too!

Moon Mode

What Happens at Night?

When the sun goes below the horizon, the system switches to moon mode.

🌙

Fewer Lights

Instead of all 8 lights, only 1 or 2 bulbs turn on — the ones closest to where the moon actually is. This keeps things calm and cozy at night.

😶

Much Dimmer

Moon mode runs at about 60% of normal brightness. The moon is way dimmer than the sun, so the lights match!

🎨

Blue-White Color

Instead of warm Kelvin colors, moon mode uses cool blue-white RGB colors — like real moonlight.

Moon Phase = Brightness

The moon's phase (how much of it is lit up) controls how bright the lights get. Full moon = brightest. New moon = dimmest.

How it decides

The system uses a space math library called PyEphem — the same kind of math NASA uses — to calculate exactly where the moon is and how full it is. Pretty cool for a light bulb, right?

A Day In The Life

What Happens From Sunrise to Bedtime?

🌅 6:30 AM — Sunrise

Wake-Up Glow

The sun peeks above the horizon in the East. The East light turns on with a warm, orange glow (2000K). Other lights stay very dim. It's like a gentle sunrise in your room.

🌤 9:00 AM — Morning

Golden Morning

The sun climbs higher and moves toward the Southeast. The light shifts to a golden color (3500K) and gets brighter. The SE and E lights are the brightest now.

☀️ 12:00 PM — Noon

Full Brightness

The sun is high in the South. All lights are at their brightest and coolest white (6500K). The South light leads, with brightness spreading evenly to nearby lights.

🌇 6:00 PM — Sunset

Evening Warmth

The sun sinks toward the West. Colors return to warm orange (2000K). The West light is brightest as the sun dips below the horizon. Cozy vibes!

🌙 9:00 PM — Night

Moon Takes Over

The system switches to Moon Mode. Only 1-2 lights glow with a soft blue-white color, following the moon's actual position. Super peaceful for bedtime.

Putting It All Together

So How Does Sun Fixture Work?

1

Check the Sky

Every 60 seconds, the system asks: "Where is the sun (or moon) right now?" It uses your location and math to figure it out.

2

Pick the Color

Based on how high the sun is: low sun = warm orange, high sun = cool white. At night, it uses blue-white moonlight colors.

3

Aim the Brightness

The light closest to the sun's direction gets the brightest. Lights on the opposite side get dim or turn off. It's like a spotlight that follows the sun.

4

Update the Bulbs

Each bulb receives its own personalized color and brightness, and smoothly fades to the new setting in about 1 second.

5

Repeat Forever!

This happens automatically, all day, every day. And you can always grab the dimmer to adjust things yourself!

🌟 In one sentence: Sun Fixture is a system that makes your indoor lights copy the real sky — following the sun during the day and the moon at night — so your home always feels natural and connected to the outdoors.

🚀 Cool Tech Used: Python programming, astronomical math (PyEphem), Home Assistant automation, Zigbee wireless protocol, WebSocket real-time communication, and Philips Hue smart lighting. That's a lot of technology working together!
